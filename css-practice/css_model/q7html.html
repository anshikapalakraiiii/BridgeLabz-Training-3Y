The Quiet Craft of Typography

Font families, weights, sizes, and spacing in practice.

Good typography rarely calls attention to itself. It simply makes reading easier, guiding the eye from word to word and line to line without friction. This page walks through a few of the properties that make that possible.

Weight and Family

This paragraph uses a serif typeface, often chosen for long-form reading because its small strokes, called serifs, help guide the eye along a line of text.

This paragraph switches to a sans-serif typeface, cleaner and more geometric, commonly used for headings, interfaces, and shorter passages of text.

This line uses a monospaced typeface, where every character takes up the same width — useful for code and tabular data.

Size, Line Height and Letter Spacing

Tight line-height and slightly condensed letter-spacing can feel dense and efficient, but may hurt readability in long paragraphs if overused.

Looser line-height and a touch of extra letter-spacing gives text room to breathe, which is often more comfortable for longer reading passages like this one.

Highlighting Inline Text

Use mark to highlight a passage the reader should notice, strong to indicate strong importance, and em to add stress emphasis to a word or phrase.

First-letter and First-line

The opening paragraph of an article can be treated typographically with a drop cap and a styled first line, a technique borrowed from printed books and newspapers, using the ::first-letter and ::first-line pseudo-elements to style only the beginning of the text without adding extra markup.
